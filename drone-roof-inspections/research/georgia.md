## Why Georgia works

- **Storms:** NOAA recorded 619 hail events, 5,249 thunderstorm/high-wind events, and 274 tornadoes in Georgia from January 2021 to April 2026 ([source](https://secommercialroofing.com/storm-data/ga/)). Coastal counties also get hurricane wind.
- **Insurance money:** State Farm alone paid nearly **$110 million** in Georgia hail claims in 2025, averaging **$17,000 per claim** ([source](https://metroatlantaceo.com/news/2026/05/hail-claims-costly-state-georgia/)).
- **Growth:** Georgia added ~98,500 residents from 2024 to 2025. Several of the fastest-growing counties in the US are in Georgia (Jackson, Long, Dawson). New homes mean new roofs, sales, and documentation needs.
- **Thin directory competition:** National directories (AeriScout, Droners, FlyService) have only a handful of Georgia listings. Local competitors are individual businesses, mostly in metro Atlanta.

**Takeaway:** Competition is real in north metro Atlanta (Fulton, Cobb). Tier 2 suburbs and smaller metros likely have far fewer providers, which makes them easier to rank in and good targets for my own service.

## Georgia rules to know

- **Home inspector licensing is coming.** [SB 458, Home Inspector Licensing Act of Georgia](https://www.legis.ga.gov/legislation/72830) passed the Senate (Mar 6, 2026) and House (Mar 31, 2026). As amended, licensing would start **January 1, 2028**. Need to confirm the governor signed it and read the final text for exceptions.
- **Georgia already defines "home inspector"** ([O.C.G.A. § 8-3-330](https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-8/chapter-3/article-6/section-8-3-330/)) as someone who, for pay, "inspects and reports on the condition of" a home, **including the roof**, for a prospective buyer or seller. Pre-sale roof work is the area most likely to fall under this. Strong reason to use "documentation" wording and avoid condition judgments.
- **Local drone rules are mostly preempted.** Under Georgia HB 481 (2017), cities and counties generally can't make their own drone flight rules, except for ordinances adopted before April 1, 2017, enforcing FAA rules, and launching/landing on public property.
- **No flying within 400 feet of ticketed events** (HB 58, 2025). Relevant near stadiums and venues.
- **FAA Part 107** certificate is required for any paid drone work.

## Running a directory while also doing the work

Being both the directory owner and a provider in it is common, but:

- Be open about it (e.g. "operated by [my business]") so other providers and customers trust the site.
- Decide early how leads are routed: my service area goes to me, others go to listed providers.
- Keep competitor listings accurate and fair, or providers won't want to list or pay.
