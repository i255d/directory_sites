# Competition, Keywords, and Viability

Research date: October 2026

## 1. Competing directories

| Site | What it is | Size | Notes |
|------|-----------|------|-------|
| [AeriScout](https://aeriscout.com/) | Directory of drone *inspection* providers, by city | ~370 listings, 67 cities | Closest direct competitor. Inspection-focused but covers all inspection types (thermal, survey, etc.), not just roofs. Quote requests. Thin coverage per city. |
| [Find a Drone Pilot](https://findadronepilot.com/) | General drone pilot directory, by state | Unknown, many states show "no pilots yet" | Broad (LiDAR, mapping, even pet search and game recovery). $0 referral fees. |
| [DroneInvoice Pilots](https://www.droneinvoice.com/pilots) | Pilot directory attached to invoicing software | Large | Free listings for its software users. Roof work is one of many services. |
| [Droners.io](https://droners.io/) | Marketplace: post a job, pilots bid | 10,000+ pilots | Biggest general player. Takes payment through the platform. Roof inspection is one category. |
| [FlyService.ai](https://www.flyservice.ai/) | General drone service directory | ~535 listings | Positions itself as "the directory AI recommends" (targets ChatGPT/Perplexity answers). Has a roof category. |

**Takeaway:** Most competitors are *general* drone directories where roofs are one category among many. Only AeriScout focuses on inspections, and even it is not roof-specific and has thin coverage. Nobody seems to own "roof documentation for roofers, adjusters, and property managers" with deep, roof-specific filters.

## 2. Pilot networks (competitors for work, but also course content)

These companies hire independent pilots to fly roofs for insurers and large property owners:

- [Zeitview](https://www.zeitview.com/properties): 80,000-pilot global network, commercial roofs, thermal, LiDAR
- [Eberl](https://eberls.com/inspection-services/): insurance claims drone network
- [AeroClaim](https://aeroclaim.us/): insurance claims drone network
- [RAAD](https://raad.com/industries/insurance): insurance and catastrophe response
- Spexi, Bees360: also mentioned in search results
- [EagleView Assess](https://www.eagleview.com/resource/assess/): **shut down to new sales March 1, 2026.** A big player leaving this segment, so insurers using it will need alternatives.

**Takeaway:** Insurance carriers increasingly go straight to these networks, so a directory is unlikely to win big insurance-carrier business. The directory's best buyers are more likely **local roofers, public adjusters, property managers, real estate agents, and homeowners**. For the course, "how to get work from pilot networks" is a valuable lesson.

## 3. Competing courses

| Course | Price | Notes |
|--------|-------|-------|
| [Drone U: Commercial Roof Inspection Mastery](https://www.thedroneu.com/courses/commercial-roof-inspection-mastery/) | Not listed in search | 19 lessons, technical workflow, software-specific |
| [Drone U: Inspection Bundle](https://www.thedroneu.com/courses/inspections-bundle/) | $347 (residential roof course alone $57) | Roofs + cell towers + construction |
| [PHII: Drone Operations for Home Inspectors](https://www.homeinspectioninstitute.com/drone-inspection.php) | Not listed | Aimed at existing home inspectors |
| [ISPM Roof Inspection program (UK)](https://www.ispm.org.uk/Course/Details/195049) | £99–£149 | UK-focused, generic |

**Takeaway:** Existing courses mostly teach *flying and capturing data*. There seems to be room for a course focused on the **business side**: finding clients, pricing, storm response, wording and liability ("documentation, not inspection"), and getting listed or hired by networks. Price range in the market: roughly $57 to $350.

## 4. Market signals

- Market estimates range from ~$350M to ~$845M in 2026, growing roughly 15% a year (market research reports; treat these as rough).
- Demand is concentrated in storm areas: **Texas, Florida, Oklahoma**, and the Midwest hail belt.
- Startup cost for a pilot is low (under $10,000: drone, FAA Part 107 test about $175, insurance, software), which means a steady supply of new pilots, who are both listing prospects and course buyers.

## 5. Keywords

Search volume numbers were not available from web search. These need to be checked with a keyword tool (see section 6). Keywords to check, grouped by who is searching:

**People hiring (directory traffic):**
- drone roof inspection near me
- drone roof inspection [city]
- drone roof inspection cost
- roof drone inspection for insurance claim
- hail damage drone inspection
- thermal roof inspection drone
- commercial roof drone survey
- drone roof documentation (likely very low volume, see terminology note)

**People wanting to start a business (course traffic):**
- how to start a drone roof inspection business
- drone roof inspection business
- how much do drone roof inspectors make
- drone roof inspection training / course / certification
- make money with drone

## 6. How to evaluate viability

### Free tools to get keyword numbers
- **Google Keyword Planner** (free with a Google Ads account): search volume ranges and cost per click
- **Google Trends**: is interest rising or falling? Compare "drone roof inspection" vs. "drone real estate photography" etc.
- **Ubersuggest / Ahrefs free keyword generator**: limited free checks of volume and difficulty
- **Google itself**: type the keywords and look at the autocomplete suggestions and "People also ask"

### Manual checks
1. **Google 5 to 10 city searches** (e.g. "drone roof inspection Dallas", "... Tulsa", "... Des Moines"). Note who ranks on page 1. If it's small local business sites and no directories, that's a good sign. If Yelp, Angi, Thumbtack, and Droners.io own page 1, it's harder.
2. **Count local providers** in a few cities. A directory needs at least 5 to 10 real providers per city to be useful.
3. **Check cost per click** in Keyword Planner. A high cost per click (e.g. $5+) means businesses pay for these leads, which means they may pay you.
4. **Ask 5 to 10 drone pilots and roofers** whether they would pay for a featured listing or leads, and how much.

### Viability scorecard (score 1–5 each)

| Factor | Question | Score |
|--------|----------|-------|
| Search demand | Do "[service] [city]" keywords get searches across many cities? | |
| Competition | Can a new site realistically reach page 1? | |
| Supply | Are there enough providers to list in each city? | |
| Willingness to pay | Do providers already pay for ads or leads? | |
| Customer value | Is a single job worth enough ($200+) to make leads valuable? | |
| Growth | Is interest rising in Google Trends? | |
| Course fit | Does directory traffic include future pilots who could buy the course? | |
| Data access | Can provider data be gathered easily (Google Maps, FAA, websites)? | |

Score 30+ out of 40: strong. 20–30: workable with a clear angle. Under 20: reconsider.

## 7. Specific vs. broad

See the recommendation in the main project discussion. Summary:

- **Directory: stay specific.** Roof / property documentation by drone, organized by city. Clear buyers, clear search intent, and a gap in the market.
- **Content and course: go broader at the top.** Use "make money with drones" style articles to attract aspiring pilots, then lead them to roof documentation as the recommended path and to the course.
- **Leave room to expand.** Structure the site so new service categories (solar panels, gutters, real estate, construction progress) can be added later without rebuilding.
