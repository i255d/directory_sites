# Terminology: "Documentation" vs. "Inspection"

## The idea

Use the word **documentation** instead of **inspection** when describing drone roof services, for both the directory site and the course.

## Why

The word "inspection" can imply a professional judgment about a roof's condition. That may:

- **Trigger licensing rules.** Many states license home inspectors or roofing inspectors, and calling a service an "inspection" may bring it under those rules.
- **Increase liability.** If a drone pilot "inspects" a roof and misses damage, a client may argue the pilot was responsible for finding it.
- **Raise insurance questions.** Insurers may treat "inspection" work as a higher-risk professional service.

"Documentation" describes what the drone pilot actually provides: photos, video, and data that a licensed roofer, adjuster, or inspector can review and use to make the assessment.

## Example wording

| Avoid                     | Prefer                                   |
|---------------------------|------------------------------------------|
| Roof inspection           | Roof documentation                       |
| Drone inspector           | Drone documentation pilot / provider     |
| Inspection report         | Imagery report / documentation report    |
| "We found damage"         | "Images show areas for review"           |

## Other words for "documentation"

**Safe (describe capturing, not judging):** imaging, imagery, images, photos, footage, video, media, visuals, views, capture, recording, record(s), archive, snapshot, overview, perspective, vantage, coverage, visual record, photo package, property record

**Use with care (can sound like you're proving or judging something):**
- *Evidence, proof:* fine for "photo evidence of the roof on [date]", but avoid suggesting you prove storm damage or a claim
- *Report:* fine as "imagery report" or "photo report"; avoid "condition report" or "damage report"
- *Monitoring, tracking:* fine for construction progress; avoid for roof condition

**Avoid (imply professional judgment or a licensed field):** inspection, assessment, evaluation, analysis, appraisal, audit, diagnosis, certification, survey (land surveying is licensed in Georgia), condition report, damage report

## Thermal imaging wording

Thermal images show temperature differences. Reading them (e.g. "this cool spot is trapped moisture" or "this is a leak") is interpretation, which is a judgment, and there's a formal training path for it (thermography certification, e.g. Level 1 Thermographer).

- **Safe:** "thermal imagery of the roof captured on [date]", "areas of temperature difference are shown for review"
- **Avoid (unless certified and insured for it):** "moisture detected", "leak found", "wet insulation identified"
- Thermal roof capture usually happens around sunset or at night (as with BDG in Atlanta), which needs extra FAA night-flying training and lighting rules

## SEO consideration

People search for "drone roof inspection," not "drone roof documentation." Possible approach: use the search term in page titles or explanatory text where needed (for example, "Looking for a drone roof inspection? Our providers offer roof documentation..."), while describing the actual services as documentation. Decide on this during keyword research.

## Georgia specifics

- Georgia law ([O.C.G.A. § 8-3-330](https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-8/chapter-3/article-6/section-8-3-330/)) defines a "home inspector" as anyone who, for pay, inspects and reports on the condition of a home, **including the roof**, for a prospective buyer or seller.
- [SB 458](https://www.legis.ga.gov/legislation/72830) (Home Inspector Licensing Act) passed both chambers in March 2026, with licensing starting January 1, 2028. Confirm final status.
- Local example of good wording: [One Sol Aerial](https://www.onesolfilmco.com/aerial/inspections) says "We provide visual documentation... we don't diagnose or certify conditions."
