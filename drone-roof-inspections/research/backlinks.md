# Backlinks Plan

## What backlinks are

A **backlink** is a link on someone else's website that points to my site. Example: a Lawrenceville roofing company's website says "Need drone photos of your roof? Find a pilot at GeorgiaDroneFinder.com" with a clickable link.

Google treats backlinks like **votes of trust**. More links from real, relevant, local websites usually means higher rankings.

- **Referring domains** = how many different websites link to me. 10 links from 10 sites is worth more than 10 links from 1 site.
- **Ahrefs Keyword Difficulty** is based on how many referring domains the top 10 results have. Low KD means few links are needed to compete.
- **Quality beats quantity.** One link from a local newspaper or chamber of commerce is worth more than 100 links from spammy sites.
- **Relevance matters.** Links from Georgia sites and from roofing, real estate, insurance, and drone sites count most.

## What to avoid

These can get the site **penalized or removed from Google**:

- Buying links or "SEO packages" promising hundreds of backlinks
- Link farms and private blog networks
- Spammy directories that list anything for a fee
- Swapping links in bulk ("I'll link to you if you link to me") with unrelated sites
- Comment spam on blogs and forums

## How to get backlinks for this directory

### 1. From the providers I list (easiest)

- Email every listed business: "You're listed on GeorgiaDroneFinder.com. Claim your free profile."
- Offer a **"Featured on GeorgiaDroneFinder" badge** they can put on their website, linking back to their profile
- Verified or featured providers are more likely to show off their listing
- Each provider with a website is a potential link: dozens of local, relevant links over time
- Proof it works: DroneInvoice's real links (DR 31) come largely from pilots linking to their own profile ("View my drone work on DroneInvoice"), plus drone forums and blogs

### 2. Local business listings (citations)

Create profiles for the directory and my business:

- Google Business Profile (my business; directories usually don't qualify)
- Bing Places
- Apple Business Connect
- Yelp, BBB, Nextdoor business page
- Local chambers of commerce (e.g. Gwinnett, Barrow, Jackson, Hall). Membership usually includes a directory listing with a link.
- Facebook and LinkedIn pages

These are basic trust signals and help my own business appear in Google Maps.

### 3. Partners who serve the same customers

Businesses that work with homeowners and property owners but don't fly drones:

- **Roofing companies:** "Need aerial documentation? We work with these pilots"
- **Real estate agents and brokerages:** resource pages for sellers
- **Insurance agents:** "What to do after a storm" pages
- **Home builders and property managers**
- **HOAs:** community resource pages
- **Home inspectors:** drone capture for roofs they can't safely walk

Approach: offer something useful to their customers (a free guide, a storm checklist, discounted drone photos for their clients), not just "please link to me."

### 4. Content worth linking to (linkable assets)

Pages other sites want to reference:

- **Georgia hail and storm map by county** (from NOAA data): roofers, insurance agents, and local news link to data like this
- **Storm reports after big hail events:** "Hail hit these Gwinnett neighborhoods on [date]", shared by local news, Facebook groups, and roofers
- **"What drone photography costs in Georgia" guide** with real price ranges
- **"What to do after a hail storm in Georgia" checklist**
- **"Drone documentation vs. roof inspection" explainer**
- **Free tool:** look up hail history by ZIP code

### 5. Local news and media

- Local outlets (county newspapers, Patch, local TV websites) cover storms. A storm data page or a quote from a local drone pilot can earn a mention and link.
- Journalist request services connect experts with reporters looking for quotes: [Qwoted](https://www.qwoted.com/), [Featured](https://featured.com/), and similar. Answer questions about drones, storms, and real estate.
- Pitch a local story: "Dacula drone pilot maps hail damage patterns across north Georgia"

### 6. Community involvement

- **Sponsor** a local youth sports team, school event, or charity run. Sponsor pages usually link to sponsors.
- **Speak** at a realtor office meeting, HOA meeting, or chamber event.
- **Volunteer drone footage** for a local nonprofit, church, or city event (with permission and legal flying). They often credit with a link.

### 7. The drone community

- Georgia drone pilot groups and associations
- Guest articles on drone blogs: "How I built a local drone business in Georgia"
- Later: the e-book and course will naturally attract links from pilots and drone sites

### 8. Copy what works for competitors

- With Ahrefs (free backlink checker or one month of Lite), look up who links to AeriScout, FlyService.ai, Find a Drone Pilot, and Georgia drone businesses
- Any site that links to them (e.g. a "drone resources" page) may link to me too

### 9. Unlinked mentions

- Search Google for "GeorgiaDroneFinder" now and then
- If a site mentions the directory without linking, politely ask them to add the link

## How the two sites help each other

- My business site (GeorgiaDroneImaging.com) links to the directory, and the directory lists my business with a link back
- Fine to do, but this alone doesn't count for much. Outside links matter most.
