# Capturing Footage vs. Judging Condition

Research date: October 2026

## Short answer

You do **not** have to judge the roof's condition. You can be the "capture" person: fly the drone, collect organized photos and video, and deliver them. Someone else (a roofer, insurance adjuster, home inspector, engineer, or software) does the judging.

This is a normal, established role. Big pilot networks like Zeitview, Eberl, and AeroClaim work exactly this way: pilots capture, and someone else reviews.

## Who judges what

| Role | What they decide | Licensed in GA? |
|------|------------------|-----------------|
| **Drone pilot (me)** | Nothing about condition. Captures and delivers imagery. | FAA Part 107 only |
| **Roofer** | Roof condition, what's damaged, repair vs. replace, cost | Contractor licensing for larger jobs |
| **Insurance adjuster** (works for the insurer) | Whether the damage is covered, cause of loss, payout | Yes |
| **Public adjuster** (works for the homeowner) | Negotiates the claim for the homeowner | Yes |
| **Home inspector** | Condition of a home for a buyer or seller | Licensing starts 2028 (SB 458) |
| **Forensic engineer** | Engineering conclusions, cause of failure | Yes (PE license) |
| **HAAG-certified inspector** | Storm damage identification (training credential, not a license) | No, it's a certification |

**Lines not to cross:** Don't tell a homeowner "your roof needs replacing" or "insurance will cover this." Don't negotiate with insurers for a homeowner, because that is public adjusting and requires a license.

## What "judging condition" involves

A roofer or adjuster looking at roof images checks for:

- **Hail damage:** round bruises or dents on shingles, granule loss in spots, dents on vents, gutters, and metal
- **Wind damage:** lifted, creased, or missing shingles, exposed nail heads
- **Wear and aging:** cracked or curling shingles, bald spots, overall granule loss
- **Flashing and penetrations:** loose flashing around chimneys, skylights, and vents; cracked pipe boots
- **Water problems:** ponding on flat roofs, staining, trapped moisture (seen with thermal cameras)
- **Other:** debris, tree damage, sagging, clogged gutters, moss or algae

Then they decide **what caused it** (storm vs. age), **how bad it is**, and **what it will cost to fix**. That's the part that takes training and carries liability.

## What I need to capture so someone else can judge

A standard capture package:

1. **Overview shots:** all four sides of the house plus a straight-down shot of the whole roof
2. **Every roof slope:** each face of the roof, photographed fully
3. **Close-ups:** ridges, valleys, edges, chimneys, skylights, vents, pipe boots, flashing
4. **Soft metals:** gutters, downspouts, vents, AC units (hail dents show up clearly here)
5. **Problem areas:** anything the client points out
6. **Optional:** thermal images (needs a thermal drone), video walkthrough, 3D model

Deliver it organized by address and date, with photos labeled by roof area. Include a statement like: *"This is visual documentation only. It is not an inspection or assessment of condition."*

## Apps that analyze the footage

Many apps take drone photos and use AI to flag possible damage, measure the roof, and build a report. Typical workflow: fly (sometimes the app flies the drone automatically) → upload photos → app processes them → report with measurements and highlighted areas.

| App | What it does | Notes |
|-----|-------------|-------|
| [Loveland Innovations IMGING](https://www.lovelandinnovations.com/roofing/) | Automated flight, AI damage detection, measurements, reports. **IMGING Flex** accepts photos from any drone with GPS data. | Automated flight averages under 6 minutes per house. Exports to Xactimate (the estimating software insurers use). |
| [DroneDeploy](https://www.dronedeploy.com/solutions/roofing/) | Mapping, 3D models, measurements, thermal, AI "Inspection agent" that flags defects | Strong for commercial roofs and construction. Pricing by quote. |
| [EagleView](https://www.eagleview.com/) | Roof measurement reports | Its drone program (Assess) stopped new sales March 2026. Measurement reports continue. |
| DJI Terra | 3D models and maps from DJI drones | Best with DJI enterprise drones |
| Pointivo | Commercial roof analytics | Commercial focus |
| Hover | 3D models from smartphone photos (no drone) | Competitor to drone work for simple houses |

**What the AI does:** It highlights *possible* damage and measures the roof. It does not make the final call. A roofer or adjuster still reviews it.

**Caution for my business:** If I hand a client an AI report that says "hail damage detected," I may be seen as judging condition. Options:

- Deliver **raw organized photos only** (lowest liability), or
- Use measurement-only reports (roof size, pitch, area), which are facts rather than judgments, or
- Sell to **roofers and adjusters** who run the AI and make the call themselves.

## Business models that fit "capture only"

1. **Subcontract for roofers:** Roofers send me addresses, I fly and deliver photos, and they judge and sell the job. Steady B2B work.
2. **Subcontract for adjusters and pilot networks:** Zeitview, Eberl, AeroClaim, and independent adjusting firms hire pilots per job.
3. **Homeowner documentation:** Pre-storm baselines and post-storm photo packages. The homeowner gives them to their roofer or insurer.
4. **Partner with a judge:** Team up with a HAAG-certified roofer or home inspector. I capture, they assess, and we split the fee.
5. **Measurement reports:** Roof size and pitch reports for roofers ordering materials. Facts, not judgments.
