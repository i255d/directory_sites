# Drone Crop Spraying in Georgia: First Look

Researched October 3, 2026. Sources linked inline. Prices and counts are from operator directories and vendors, so treat them as estimates.

## The market

- Georgia: ~42,400 farms, ~10.2M farmland acres, over $10B a year in farm revenue ([NuWay Ag](https://nuwayag.com/pages/georgia-state-guide)).
- Main sprayed crops: **cotton ~1.2M acres** (#2 in US), **peanuts ~800K acres** (#1, about half of US supply), corn, soybeans, **pecans ~180K acres**, blueberries ~25K acres, central Georgia peaches ([AgDroneDirectory](https://agdronedirectory.com/states/georgia)).
- Operators: roughly **25 listed** in Georgia ([AgDroneDirectory](https://agdronedirectory.com/guides/agricultural-drone-spraying-statistics-2026)); one vendor estimates 0–50 active ([NuWay Ag](https://nuwayag.com/pages/georgia-state-guide)). Vendors call competition "extremely low," but vendors are selling drones.
- Existing Georgia operators include AgriFlyer (South GA), a Reinke irrigation dealer in Smithville (sells DJI and sprays), KDB Land and Air, Southern Skies Ag Drone, Pegasus Robotics, Ranch & Row (FL/GA), Osprey Agri Drones (network).

### Where the work is (important for Dacula)

The big row-crop acres are in **South and Southwest Georgia** (peanuts, cotton, corn), roughly 150–250 miles from Dacula. Inside the 150-mile area, likely work is:
- Pasture and hay field weed control (common in North and Middle Georgia)
- Middle Georgia row crops and peaches (Macon/Fort Valley area)
- Pecans and orchards farther south
- Ponds and aquatic weeds, forestry/pine, right-of-way, mosquito control (each has its own license category)

## Prices farmers pay (application only; farmer supplies chemical)

| Work | Rate per acre |
|------|---------------|
| Georgia overall | $16–$26 |
| Cotton (incl. defoliant) | $14–$20; tank mixes $18–$25 |
| Pecans, blueberries, orchards | $22–$26 |
| Southeast range | $16–$28 |

Common terms: 40-acre minimums, travel surcharges, big blocks (500+ acres) negotiated toward the low end. ([AgDroneDirectory cotton](https://agdronedirectory.com/states/georgia/crops/cotton))

A spray drone crew can cover roughly **300–600 acres a day** in good conditions. Example only: 400 acres × $17 = **~$6,800 a day**. The catch is that spray days are limited by season, weather (wind under ~10 mph), and crop timing (cotton defoliant Sept–Oct; fungicides on peanuts in summer).

## Licenses and approvals (all required)

**Federal (FAA)**
1. **Part 107** remote pilot certificate.
2. **Section 44807 exemption.** Most spray drones weigh over 55 lb loaded, so they fly under Part 91/137 with an exemption. The FAA now has a pre-approved list of spray drones. FAA says to file at least 120 days ahead; Hylio says ~2 months in practice. ([FAA](https://www.faa.gov/uas/advanced_operations/dispensing_chemicals), [Hylio](https://www.hyl.io/faa-information))
3. **Part 137 Agricultural Aircraft Operator Certificate** (Form 8710-3), after the exemption. ~2 weeks per Hylio.
4. Aircraft registration (N-number for 55 lb+).
5. Hylio lists a **3rd-class airman medical** as required; confirm with FAA for the specific exemption.

**Georgia (Department of Agriculture)**
1. **Commercial Pesticide Applicator License**: general standards exam + at least one crop/site category, plus **Category 34 (Aerial Methods)**, which requires proof of FAA certification. Exams ~$45 each; license ~$90 for 5 years. ([GDA categories](https://www.agr.georgia.gov/pesticide-applicator-license-categories))
2. **Pesticide Contractor License** for the business, required to spray anyone else's land for pay. Needs a certified applicator on staff, **financial responsibility (insurance)**, annual renewal. ([GDA contractors](https://agr.ga.gov/pesticide-contractors))

## Equipment

- Spray drones run roughly **$20K–$40K+ each**; a working setup (drone, extra batteries, generator, mixing equipment, trailer, truck) is often **$50K–$80K+**.
- **DJI Agras** (T50, T55, T100) is the market leader (~4 in 5 US spray drones). On Dec 22, 2025 the FCC added new foreign-made drones to its Covered List. Models authorized **before** that date can still be sold and flown. A **July 2026 FCC proposal** could stop future sales of some current models and make parts harder to get. Existing owners can keep flying. ([DroneRanker](https://www.droneranker.com/agricultural-drone-ban-us-spray-drones/), [AgFunder](https://agfundernews.com/new-fcc-proposal-could-retroactively-ban-some-existing-ag-spray-drones-warns-dji))
- **US-made alternatives:** Hylio (Texas, ~2,500/yr; helps with FAA paperwork free), Ceres Air, Revolution Drones, Exedy. Usually more expensive; some still use foreign parts.
- **Risk:** buying DJI now could leave you without parts or replacement aircraft later.

## Big risks

- **Chemical liability:** drift onto neighbors' crops, gardens, bees, or homes can mean lawsuits and license action.
- **Capital:** $50K–$80K+ up front, plus insurance, batteries, and repairs.
- **Regulation:** FCC drone rules are changing; FAA exemption and Part 137 paperwork; Georgia aerial and contractor licenses.
- **Season and weather:** income concentrated in a few months; wind and rain cancel days.
- **Distance:** main row-crop acres are far from Dacula.
- **Relationships:** farmers buy from people they know; breaking in takes time.
