## Water damage in Buford

Buford is two places. On one side is the mall: Mall of Georgia, Friendship Road, and I-985. When people here say "the Mall," they mean Mall of Georgia. That side is mostly houses, townhomes, and apartments built from the late 1990s on. On the other side is Lake Lanier and Buford Dam, with older cottages from the 1960s and newer custom houses on the shoreline. Old Buford, around Main Street, is older still.

Houses on the mall side are now 15 to 30 years old. That is the age when the water heater, the washer hose, and the refrigerator line start to fail. So the usual call there is a leak, not a flood.

The lake side is different. The houses vary a lot in age, and the oldest cottages may have older plumbing than anything built since. Many waterfront lots are on septic, not city sewer. That matters, because a backup is a different problem and a different first call.

What is under the house matters too. In the mall corridor you find both slab houses and houses with basements. On the hills near the lake, a walkout basement or a crawl space is common. In Old Buford, older houses more often sit over a crawl space.

That is why the first questions are about your house. When you call or send the form, tell us where the water came from, which rooms are wet, and how long it has been there. Tell us if the house has a basement, a crawl space, or a slab. If it is on septic, say that too. If you are not sure, say so. That is enough to start.

Storms do some damage. In a January 2022 storm, trees and wires came down on roads across Buford, and one landed on a house. A damaged roof can leak into the attic for days before the stain shows up on the ceiling.

## Common causes in Buford

- **Water heater.** The tank leaks or lets go in a garage or closet. This is common in houses from the late 1990s through the 2010s.
- **Refrigerator or washer line.** The hose cracks. Water slides under the floor, and you smell it before you see it.
- **Shared-wall leak.** In a townhome, a neighbor's leak can come through the wall you share.
- **Septic backup.** On a lake lot, wastewater can back up into a tub or floor drain. This is sewage.
- **Old pipes.** Older cottages may have pipe that has been in the walls for decades. See the question below.
- **Burst pipe.** After a hard freeze, a pipe can split in a wall or an attic. Gwinnett had a lot of these in December 2022.
- **Storm water.** Rain gets in through a window, a door, or a roof damaged by wind or a falling tree.
- **Sewage, a sagging ceiling, or wet outlets.** Do not clean these up yourself. Call.

## If water is in the house now

1. Shut the water off, if you can do it safely. Stay away from wet outlets.
2. Take photos of the water, the room, and where it started.
3. Sewage, a sagging ceiling, or wet outlets: call (404) 882-7393 or send the form.
4. Already stopped a small drip? Use the [small-leak steps](/small-leaks).

Call your insurance company when you can. If you plan to file a claim, do not start cutting out drywall first.

Do not wait until tomorrow to dry things out. Wet drywall and flooring get worse overnight, and mold can start in 24 to 48 hours.

## Questions from Buford homeowners

**Our house is on septic and sewage is coming up in the tub. Who do we call?**
Stay out of the water and do not use the plumbing. Sewage is dirty water, and it should not be cleaned up by hand. You need two calls. A septic company has to find out why it backed up. Call us for the part inside the house, such as soaked floors, drywall, and carpet. Tell us it is septic when you call.

**Water is coming up in the street near my house. Do I call the city or the county?**
It depends on who sends your water bill. The City of Buford has its own water department, and some parcels on the edge of town are served by Gwinnett County. Check your bill. If water is also inside your house, shut off the main valve, take photos, and call us about the wet part inside.

**We are only at the lake house on weekends. How do we keep a leak from running all week?**
Shut off the main valve when you leave. That one step stops most leaks from running while no one is there. Turn the water heater to vacation mode or off, and ask someone to check the house if you are away for a long time. If you come back to wet floors, take photos before you move anything and call.

**My townhome neighbor had a leak and water is coming through the shared wall. What do I do?**
Take photos of the wall and floor on your side, and write down when you first saw it. Tell your neighbor and your management company or HOA, in writing. Do not open the wall yourself. Water can sit inside a shared wall long after the wall looks dry. Call, and tell us it is a shared wall.

**My cottage was built in the 1960s. What is in the walls?**
It depends on the house. Older cottages may have copper, older steel pipe, or gray plastic pipe, and some may have been repiped. You cannot tell by looking at the neighborhood. Find the main shut-off, make sure it turns, and look at the pipes near the water heater. If you are not sure, ask a plumber.

Questions about drying time, insurance, or whether you have to hire the company that calls are answered on [FAQ](/faq) and [Insurance](/insurance).

## Shared (keep short on this URL)

**What a job looks like.** A crew checks the house, pulls out standing water, dries the structure, and replaces what can't be saved. You should be shown moisture readings, not just told it looks dry. How long it takes depends on the job. [How it works](/how-it-works).

**Clean water, dirty water, sewage.** A broken supply line is not the same as a washer overflow or a sewer or septic backup. Say which one it is on the form.

**Insurance.** A sudden burst pipe or a failed appliance is often covered. A slow leak, outside water, or sewage may not be unless you bought extra coverage. Take photos first. Don't tear out drywall to "help." Ask your insurer. [Insurance](/insurance).
