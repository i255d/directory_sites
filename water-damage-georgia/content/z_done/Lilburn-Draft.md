## Water damage in Lilburn

Lilburn has some of the oldest houses in this part of Gwinnett. The typical house was built around 1982, so many are more than 40 years old. The daily roads are Highway 29, which is Lawrenceville Highway, Killian Hill Road, and Five Forks Trickum Road. The Yellow River runs along the edge of town.

The houses along the river, in Rivermist and Rivercliff, are from the late 1970s and 1980s. Many are two-story houses with full basements, and many are on septic. Downtown Lilburn is older and more likely to sit over a crawl space. Newer infill streets, built in the 2020s, have smaller lots and more houses on a slab. That is a different Lilburn from the river houses, and a leak does different things in each.

A house that old has had time to wear out. Water heaters have been replaced, some more than once. The original supply lines and the outdoor faucets are often still there, and they fail.

Pipes are a bigger question here than in newer cities. Homes built from the late 1970s to the mid-1990s may have gray plastic pipe, called polybutylene, which is known to fail. You cannot tell from the neighborhood. Look under the sinks and near the water heater, and ask a plumber if you are not sure.

That is why the first questions are about your house. When you call or send the form, tell us where the water came from, which rooms are wet, and how long it has been there. Tell us if the house has a basement, a crawl space, or a slab. If you are on septic, say so. If you are not sure, say so. That is enough to start.

Lilburn has creeks too. During Hurricane Helene in 2024, Hillcrest Road was closed where it crosses Jackson Creek, and the Lilburn Greenway Trail was closed. Sweetwater Creek at Club Drive reached 12.3 feet, and the weather service put out a flood warning. Most of this is road and park flooding, not houses. But if you live near a creek, that is worth knowing.

Storms do some damage. Trees and wires have come down around Mountain Park and other parts of Lilburn. A damaged roof can leak into the attic for days before the stain shows up on the ceiling.

## Where people say they live

Lilburn has two ZIP codes, 30047 and 30048.

**Downtown Lilburn.** Small-town streets with older houses. Crawl spaces are common.

**Rivermist and Rivercliff.** Along the Yellow River. Mostly two-story houses from the late 1970s and 1980s, with full basements. Many are on septic.

**Mountain Park.** Houses from the 1970s and 1980s on bigger lots, off Five Forks Trickum Road. The area goes back to the 1960s.

**Killian Hill.** Ranch, split-level, and two-story houses from the 1970s to the 1990s. This is the daily-road Lilburn.

**Cherokee Woods and Hanarry.** Established neighborhoods with older houses.

**Newer infill.** Smaller lots and newer houses from the 2020s, mostly on a slab.

## Common causes in Lilburn

- **Water heater.** The tank leaks or lets go in a garage, closet, or basement. Houses from the 1970s and 1980s are on a later replacement cycle.
- **Old supply lines and outdoor faucets.** Original lines and hose bibs wear out and leak inside the wall.
- **Gray plastic pipe.** Some houses from the late 1970s to the mid-1990s may have polybutylene pipe that is known to fail.
- **Basement water.** A leak upstairs or water from outside ends up in a full basement. A basement cleanup takes longer than a one-room leak.
- **Water under new flooring.** In a newer slab house, water can run under vinyl plank before anyone sees it.
- **Burst pipe.** After a hard freeze, a pipe can split in a wall or an attic. Gwinnett had a lot of these in December 2022.
- **Septic backup.** On river lots, wastewater can back up into a floor drain or tub. This is sewage.
- **Storm water.** Rain gets in through a window, a door, or a roof damaged by wind or a falling tree.
- **Sewage, a sagging ceiling, or wet outlets.** Do not clean these up yourself. Call.

## If water is in the house now

1. Shut the water off, if you can do it safely. Stay away from wet outlets.
2. Take photos of the water, the room, and where it started.
3. Sewage, a sagging ceiling, or wet outlets: call (404) 882-7393 or send the form.
4. Already stopped a small drip? Use the [small-leak steps](/small-leaks).

Call your insurance company when you can. If you plan to file a claim, do not start cutting out drywall first.

Do not wait until tomorrow to dry things out. Wet drywall and flooring get worse overnight, and mold can start in 24 to 48 hours.

## Questions from Lilburn homeowners

**We repiped the house years ago. Is a leak still likely?**
Yes, though not from the old pipe. New pipe does not fix the water heater, the washer hose, the refrigerator line, or the outdoor faucets, and those still fail. If you had gray plastic pipe replaced, that was one of the bigger risks in a house this age. Keep the shut-off easy to reach, and look at the hoses and the water heater every year or so.

**The road to my house is closed by water. What do I do?**
Do not drive through water on the road. Even a few inches can hide a washed-out edge. Tell us about the closed road when you call, and which way in is open. If water is inside your house, shut off the main valve and take photos. In Helene, Hillcrest Road was closed over Jackson Creek for a time, so a closed road can happen here.

**Our basement is full of boxes and stuff, and now it is wet. What do I save?**
Take photos of everything before you move anything. Move wet items you can safely carry to a dry spot off the floor. Make a list of what was wet, since your insurer will want one. Do not throw things away before the photos. Photos, papers, and anything that matters to you are worth keeping apart. Tell us when you call, and we will point you to the right help.

**The floor in my newer house looks fine, but it feels bumpy and smells damp. What is it?**
Water can run under vinyl plank floors and sit there. The planks may lift at the edges, feel soft, or smell musty. If you see any of that, find the source if you can, such as a fridge line, a toilet supply, or a dishwasher. Take photos and call. Do not pull the floor up yourself if you plan to file a claim.

**What a job looks like.** A crew checks the house, pulls out standing water, dries the structure, and replaces what can't be saved. You should be shown moisture readings, not just told it looks dry. How long it takes depends on the job. [How it works](/how-it-works).

**Clean water, dirty water, sewage.** A broken supply line is not the same as a washer overflow or a sewer or septic backup. Say which one it is on the form.

**Insurance.** A sudden burst pipe or a failed appliance is often covered. A slow leak, outside water, or sewage may not be unless you bought extra coverage. Take photos first. Don't tear out drywall to "help." Ask your insurer. [Insurance](/insurance).
