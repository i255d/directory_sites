## Water damage in Grayson

Most people who say they live in Grayson do not live in the city. The city of Grayson is tiny, about 4,700 people. Most of the houses with a Grayson address are in unincorporated Gwinnett County, in subdivisions out along Highway 20, Rosebud Road, and Pharrs Road. State Route 20 is even called two things. It is Grayson Highway on one side of town and Loganville Highway on the other.

The big subdivisions are mostly from the 2000s. Wheatfields and Great River at Tribble Mill were built then, with two- and three-story houses and swim-and-tennis clubs. So many homes here are 20 to 25 years old. The water heater and the hoses from those first years are now old, which is why the usual call is a leak, not a flood.

What is under the house matters. Some of the 2000s houses have basements, and the older houses in and around the town are more likely to sit over a crawl space. On a slab, water spreads across the floor. Tell us what you have, and if you are not sure, say so.

Grayson does have a river, the Alcovy. The gauge people hear about on the news is the one at New Hope Road. Flood stage is 9 feet. During Hurricane Helene in 2024, it reached about 10.6 feet, which the weather service called minor flooding. At 11 feet, the weather service says the water reaches the bottom of the bridge. That number is for the river at the bridge. It does not measure your street, and most houses here are not next to the river.

That is why the first questions are about your house. When you call or send the form, tell us where the water came from, which rooms are wet, and how long it has been there. Also tell us your street and nearest crossroad. Mail addresses around here can be confusing.

Storms do some damage. In a January 2022 storm, a tree came down at Chandler Road and Tribble Creek Drive. A damaged roof can leak into the attic for days before the stain shows up on the ceiling.

## Where people say they live

Grayson has one ZIP code, 30017.

**Uptown and the Railyard.** The old town center, with new shops and restaurants. Older houses nearby.

**Wheatfields and Wheatfields Reserve.** Craftsman and traditional two- and three-story houses from the 2000s, with a swim-and-tennis club. Some have basements.

**Great River at Tribble Mill.** About 525 houses from the 2000s, near Tribble Mill Park. Tribble Mill is a county park, not a city park.

**Pharrs Road.** A mix of older and newer houses.

**The Highway 20 corridor.** Everything, from older houses to newer streets. It is not one neighborhood.

## Common causes in Grayson

- **Water heater.** The tank leaks or lets go in a garage or closet. This is common in houses from the 2000s.
- **Refrigerator or washer line.** The hose cracks. Water slides under the floor, and you smell it before you see it.
- **Burst pipe.** After a hard freeze, a pipe can split in a wall or an attic. Gwinnett had a lot of these in December 2022.
- **Outdoor faucet or irrigation line.** A broken line near the house can send water along the foundation and into a basement or crawl space.
- **Storm water.** Rain gets in through a window, a door, or a roof damaged by wind or a falling tree.
- **Sewage, a sagging ceiling, or wet outlets.** Do not clean these up yourself. Call.

## If water is in the house now

1. Shut the water off, if you can do it safely. Stay away from wet outlets.
2. Take photos of the water, the room, and where it started.
3. Sewage, a sagging ceiling, or wet outlets: call (404) 882-7393 or send the form.
4. Already stopped a small drip? Use the [small-leak steps](/small-leaks).

Call your insurance company when you can. If you plan to file a claim, do not start cutting out drywall first.

Do not wait until tomorrow to dry things out. Wet drywall and flooring get worse overnight, and mold can start in 24 to 48 hours.

## Questions from Grayson homeowners

**My mail says one town, but I live near Grayson. What address do I give?**
Give the street address and ZIP exactly as they are on your mail, and then tell us the nearest crossroad, such as Rosebud Road, Pharrs Road, or Highway 20. Mailing towns around here do not always match where you live, and Highway 20 changes names from one side of town to the other. That helps the crew find the right house.

**The news said the Alcovy is at 10 feet. Does that mean my house will flood?**
Not by itself. That number is for the river at the New Hope Road bridge. It does not measure your street. If you live near the river, watch the gauge when heavy rain is coming, and move anything valuable up off the floor. Ask your insurer about outside water coverage before the storm, not after. If water does get in, take photos and call.

**An outdoor faucet froze and water ran inside the wall. What do I do?**
Shut off the main valve first. A split faucet can run for hours before anyone sees it, and the water goes into the wall and under the floor. Take photos, do not open the wall yourself, and call. Tell us how long it may have been running.

**A sprinkler line broke near the house. Can that cause water damage inside?**
Yes. If you have an irrigation system, a broken line near the foundation can send water along the wall and into a basement or crawl space. Turn off the irrigation system, look for wet carpet or a musty smell in the lower level, and call if you find water.

Questions about drying time, insurance, or whether you have to hire the company that calls are answered on [FAQ](/faq) and [Insurance](/insurance).

## Shared (keep short on this URL)

**What a job looks like.** A crew checks the house, pulls out standing water, dries the structure, and replaces what can't be saved. You should be shown moisture readings, not just told it looks dry. How long it takes depends on the job. [How it works](/how-it-works).

**Clean water, dirty water, sewage.** A broken supply line is not the same as a washer overflow or a sewer backup. Say which one it is on the form.

**Insurance.** A sudden burst pipe or a failed appliance is often covered. A slow leak, outside water, or sewage may not be unless you bought extra coverage. Take photos first. Don't tear out drywall to "help." Ask your insurer. [Insurance](/insurance).
