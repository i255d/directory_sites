## Water damage in Duluth

Duluth is two places. Downtown is Town Green and Parsons Alley, which were rebuilt in the 2010s with shops, restaurants, apartments, and some older houses around them. The other Duluth is around Pleasant Hill Road and I-85, where Gwinnett Place Mall used to be. The mall has been closed since 2021, and people still give directions off it. Peachtree Industrial, which everyone calls PIB, runs through the middle.

Duluth has more attached housing than most of Gwinnett. There are townhomes, condos, and apartments along PIB and Pleasant Hill, and a lot of them have a unit upstairs and a unit below. That changes the usual call. It is often a leak in one unit that ends up in another.

The typical house in Duluth was built around 1992. That is old enough for the water heater, the washer hose, and the supply lines to be on their second round or past it. In the single-family houses, the usual call is a water heater or a hose. In the attached housing, it is water that came through a ceiling.

What is under the house matters too. Around Pleasant Hill and Gwinnett Place, many older homes and condos are on a slab or a crawl space. In the gated country club neighborhood, basements are common. Tell us what you have, and if you are not sure, say so.

That is why the first questions are about your home. When you call or send the form, tell us where the water came from, which rooms are wet, and how long it has been there. If it is a townhome or condo, tell us which floor you are on and whether the water came from a unit next to you or above you.

Storms do some damage. In a January 2022 storm, a tree came down near Cardinal Lake. And during Hurricane Helene in 2024, photos showed water on Indian Trail near I-85. A damaged roof can leak into the attic for days before the stain shows up on the ceiling.

## Where people say they live

Duluth has several ZIP codes. 30096 and 30095 cover most of the city. The gated country club neighborhood is usually listed as 30097.

**Town Green and Parsons Alley.** The rebuilt downtown. Apartments, shops, and some older houses nearby.

**Pleasant Hill and Gwinnett Place.** Condos, townhomes, and single-family houses from the 1980s and 1990s. Many people here rent.

**Peachtree Industrial (PIB).** Townhomes and two-story houses. This is where you see the most upstairs-and-downstairs leaks.

**Sugarloaf Country Club and Sugarloaf Park.** Gated estate houses from the late 1990s and 2000s, with basements. Sugarloaf Park is smaller and next door. This is not the same place as Sugarloaf Mills, which is in Lawrenceville.

**Cardinal Lake Estates.** Older, with more than 650 modest houses around a lake.

## Common causes in Duluth

- **Leak from the unit above.** A burst supply line, an overflowing tub, or a failed water heater upstairs ends up in the ceiling below.
- **Water heater.** The tank leaks or lets go in a garage or closet. This is common in houses from the 1980s and 1990s.
- **Refrigerator or washer line.** The hose cracks. Water slides under the floor, and you smell it before you see it.
- **Burst pipe.** After a hard freeze, a pipe can split in a wall or an attic. In December 2022, a burst pipe left the Villas at Duluth apartments without water for days.
- **Outside water at ground level.** In heavy rain, water can come in under a door on the ground floor, especially near Indian Trail and Pleasant Hill.
- **Storm damage.** Rain gets in through a window, a door, or a roof damaged by wind or a falling tree.
- **Sewage, a sagging ceiling, or wet outlets.** Do not clean these up yourself. Call.

## If water is in the house now

1. Shut the water off, if you can do it safely. Stay away from wet outlets.
2. Take photos of the water, the room, and where it started.
3. Sewage, a sagging ceiling, or wet outlets: call (404) 882-7393 or send the form.
4. Already stopped a small drip? Use the [small-leak steps](/small-leaks).

Call your insurance company when you can. If you plan to file a claim, do not start cutting out drywall first.

Do not wait until tomorrow to dry things out. Wet drywall and flooring get worse overnight, and mold can start in 24 to 48 hours.

## Questions from Duluth homeowners

**I own a townhome and the leak started in my unit. Do I call the HOA or handle it myself?**
Look at your HOA documents, or call the management company, and ask who is responsible for what. In many townhome communities the HOA handles the building outside and the owner handles the inside, but the rules differ, so ask. Take photos before anything is moved, and tell the HOA in writing if the water could reach a neighbor. Then call us for the drying. Ask your insurer too, since an HOA policy and your own policy cover different things.

**Water is coming in under the door of my ground-floor condo during a hard rain. What do I do?**
Stay away from wet outlets, and do not wade through the water. It is outside water and can be dirty. Take photos of the water line on the walls and the floor. Tell your property manager. Then call. Outside water is often covered differently from a pipe leak, so ask your insurer what your policy says.

**A pipe burst in my apartment building and the whole building lost water. What do I do?**
Ask the property manager how long it will last, and whether a restoration company has been called for the building. Take photos of any wet floor or ceiling in your own unit, even if it looks small. Do not run the taps or flush if the building is told to stay off the water. If your unit has standing water or a sagging ceiling, call us.

**My neighbor's leak is coming through my ceiling and they are not home. What now?**
Take photos, and write down the time you first saw it. Call your property manager or HOA and ask them to shut off the water to the unit above. Stay out from under a sagging ceiling. If the water keeps coming, call. Do not try to enter the other unit yourself.

Questions about drying time, insurance, or whether you have to hire the company that calls are answered on [FAQ](/faq) and [Insurance](/insurance).

## Shared (keep short on this URL)

**What a job looks like.** A crew checks the house, pulls out standing water, dries the structure, and replaces what can't be saved. You should be shown moisture readings, not just told it looks dry. How long it takes depends on the job. [How it works](/how-it-works).

**Clean water, dirty water, sewage.** A broken supply line is not the same as a washer overflow or a sewer backup. Say which one it is on the form.

**Insurance.** A sudden burst pipe or a failed appliance is often covered. A slow leak, outside water, or sewage may not be unless you bought extra coverage. Take photos first. Don't tear out drywall to "help." Ask your insurer. [Insurance](/insurance).
