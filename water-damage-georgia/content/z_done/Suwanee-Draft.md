## Water damage in Suwanee

The railroad tracks split Suwanee in two. On one side is Town Center, with the park, the fountain, the amphitheater, and City Hall. On the other is Old Town, with Main Street and the red caboose. Freight trains run through both, and people here navigate by which side of the tracks they are on.

Most of the houses outside Old Town were built from the mid-1990s on. Morningview was built around 1996 to 2000. Town Center and Shadowbrook came in the mid-2000s. So many homes are 20 to 30 years old. That is the age when the water heater, the washer hose, and the refrigerator line start to fail. The usual call is a leak, not a flood.

Old Town is different. It has older houses on small lots. Many are still on septic, not sewer. The city says there is an easy way to tell. Look at your Gwinnett County water bill. If you pay nothing for sewer, you are probably on septic.

What is under the house matters. Many houses in Morningview have basements, and some are daylight basements. Older houses in Old Town more often sit over a crawl space. On a slab, water spreads across the floor. Tell us what you have, and if you are not sure, say so.

Suwanee also has a creek. During Hurricane Helene in 2024, Suwanee Creek Park, Martin Farm Park, George Pierce Park, the greenway, and the playground behind Suwanee Elementary all flooded. If you live near the creek, that is not just a park problem.

That is why the first questions are about your house. When you call or send the form, tell us where the water came from, which rooms are wet, and how long it has been there. If you are on septic, say so.

Storms do some damage. In a January 2022 storm, trees came down in Suwanee. A damaged roof can leak into the attic for days before the stain shows up on the ceiling.

## Common causes in Suwanee

- **Water heater.** The tank leaks or lets go in a garage or closet. This is common in houses from the late 1990s and 2000s.
- **Refrigerator or washer line.** The hose cracks. Water slides under the floor, and you smell it before you see it.
- **Creek water.** After heavy rain, the creek can rise into yards and low parts of the house near the greenway.
- **Septic backup.** In Old Town, wastewater can back up into a tub or floor drain. This is sewage.
- **Burst pipe.** After a hard freeze, a pipe can split in a wall or an attic. Gwinnett had a lot of these in December 2022.
- **Storm damage.** Rain gets in through a window, a door, or a roof damaged by wind or a falling tree.
- **Sewage, a sagging ceiling, or wet outlets.** Do not clean these up yourself. Call.

## If water is in the house now

1. Shut the water off, if you can do it safely. Stay away from wet outlets.
2. Take photos of the water, the room, and where it started.
3. Sewage, a sagging ceiling, or wet outlets: call (404) 882-7393 or send the form.
4. Already stopped a small drip? Use the [small-leak steps](/small-leaks).

Call your insurance company when you can. If you plan to file a claim, do not start cutting out drywall first.

Do not wait until tomorrow to dry things out. Wet drywall and flooring get worse overnight, and mold can start in 24 to 48 hours.

## Questions from Suwanee homeowners

**How do I know if my house is on septic or sewer?**
Look at your Gwinnett County water bill. If it has no sewer charge, the house is probably on septic. This matters when you call, because a septic backup is a different problem from a sewer backup. Tell us which you have, or tell us you are not sure.

**Is there a way to tell if the creek is going to reach my house?**
Suwanee Creek has a gauge, and the National Weather Service posts forecasts for it. Flood stage is 8 feet. At 11 feet, the greenway has 1 to 3 feet of water on it, and some yards along the creek take water. Check the gauge when heavy rain is coming, and move anything valuable up off the floor. We cannot tell you about your own house. Ask your insurer about outside water coverage before the storm, not after.

**My address says Suwanee but I think I am in Forsyth County. Does that matter?**
It might. Some addresses with a Suwanee mailing address are in Forsyth County, not Gwinnett. When you call or send the form, give your street and ZIP, and say if you think you are in Forsyth. That way the right company can answer.

**I live in a gated neighborhood. How does a crew get in?**
Tell us the gate code or the guard house name when you call, or put it in the form. If the gate needs a name on the list, put the name on the list before the crew comes. If you are not home, ask a neighbor to meet them.

Questions about drying time, insurance, or whether you have to hire the company that calls are answered on [FAQ](/faq) and [Insurance](/insurance).

## Shared (keep short on this URL)

**What a job looks like.** A crew checks the house, pulls out standing water, dries the structure, and replaces what can't be saved. You should be shown moisture readings, not just told it looks dry. How long it takes depends on the job. [How it works](/how-it-works).

**Clean water, dirty water, sewage.** A broken supply line is not the same as a washer overflow or a sewer or septic backup. Say which one it is on the form.

**Insurance.** A sudden burst pipe or a failed appliance is often covered. A slow leak, outside water, or sewage may not be unless you bought extra coverage. Take photos first. Don't tear out drywall to "help." Ask your insurer. [Insurance](/insurance).
