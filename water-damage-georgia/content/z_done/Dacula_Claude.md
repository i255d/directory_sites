## What Causes Water Damage in Dacula

**Appliance and supply-line failures.** Hamilton Mill and Apalachee Farms were built mostly between the mid-1990s and the mid-2000s. Water heaters, braided washing-machine hoses, ice-maker lines, and under-sink supply lines installed in that era (or replaced since) are reaching the age where failures become common. These leaks are usually sudden, and they often happen while no one is home.

**Basements and finished lower levels.** Many Dacula homes are slab-on-grade or have crawl spaces, but some, including ranch-with-basement layouts in neighborhoods like Apalachee Crossing and basements in parts of Hamilton Mill, have finished lower levels. A leak in a basement can soak carpet, drywall, and furniture before anyone notices.

**Heavy rain and creek flooding.** The Apalachee River runs through the area, and the Alcovy River lies south of Highway 316. Federal gauge data shows the Apalachee at Fence Road peaked at 8.56 feet during the September 2009 floods. During Hurricane Helene in 2024, Gwinnett County was under a flood watch, though the county's flood concerns centered more on the south and west than on this ridge. Even so, a hard Georgia downpour can push water through window sills, sliding doors, and low spots in the yard.

**Wind, hail, and falling trees.** Storm crews responded to downed trees in Dacula during a January 2022 winter storm, and hail around 2.5 inches was reported near Dacula in 2018. Damaged roofs and flashing let rain into attics and ceilings, often days before a stain appears.

## What Happens When You Send a Request

We are a referral service, not a restoration company. Here's how it works:

1. **Tell us what happened.** Call or fill out the form with your address, what kind of water it is, and which rooms are affected.
2. **We pass it to a local restoration company.** They will call you to talk through the situation.
3. **They inspect and plan.** A good crew checks for hidden moisture in walls, flooring, and under cabinets, not just what you can see.
4. **They remove water and dry the structure.** Pumps and extractors take out standing water. Air movers and dehumidifiers then run for days, and the crew should show you moisture readings, not just say it looks dry.
5. **Cleanup and repairs.** Depending on the damage, this can include removing wet pad, drywall, or flooring.

You are never required to hire the company that calls you, and you're free to get a second opinion before any work starts.

## Quick Steps While You Wait

- If it's safe, shut off the water at the source.
- Stay out of rooms with wet outlets, a sagging ceiling, or sewage.
- Photograph the damage before you move or throw anything away.
- Call your insurance carrier when you can.
- Don't start cutting drywall if you plan to file a claim.

## Insurance and Water Damage in Dacula

Many homeowners policies cover sudden events like a burst pipe or an appliance failure. Slow leaks that went unaddressed, flooding from a creek or street, and sewer backup may not be covered unless you bought those add-ons. Only your policy can answer that, so check with your carrier. We don't file claims or bill insurance, but the restoration company that contacts you can document the damage and talk with your adjuster.

## Dacula Water Damage FAQ

**How fast will someone respond?**
It depends on the company, the time of day, and how many storms are hitting Gwinnett that night. We can't promise a specific arrival time, but emergency water is worth a call right away, because drying sooner almost always costs less.

**Can I stay in my house?**
Often yes for a clean-water leak in one room. Stay out if there's sewage, a wet electrical panel, or a ceiling coming down.

**How long does drying take?**
A single-room clean-water leak can take a couple of days of fans and dehumidifiers. Wet pad, hardwood, or wall cavities can take longer.

**How soon can mold start?**
Mold can begin to develop within 24 to 48 hours if wet materials sit, which is why waiting until morning can make the job bigger.
