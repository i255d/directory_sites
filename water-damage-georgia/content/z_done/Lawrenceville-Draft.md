## Water damage in Lawrenceville

Lawrenceville is the county seat, and it is bigger than the downtown most people picture. Downtown has two centers that locals name separately, the Square and the Lawn. The rest of the city runs out from there: Collins Hill, Sugarloaf Mills, the Old Norcross Road side, and a lot of lake and pond neighborhoods built in the late 1980s and 1990s.

Many of those houses are 25 to 40 years old. The water heater, the supply lines, and the washer hoses in them are showing it. So the usual call is a leak, not a flood. It is a water heater that let go, a soft spot in the kitchen floor, or a stain on the ceiling.

The south and west sides of the city are different. Wolf Creek and Pew Creek run through that part of town, and during Hurricane Helene in 2024 county officials watched both of them for flooding. The Collins Hill and Sugarloaf side did not get the same attention. If you live near a creek, water from outside is a real possibility, and it is handled differently from a pipe leak.

What is under the house changes where the water goes. Older houses near downtown more often sit over a crawl space. Many lake and hillside streets have basements. On a slab, water spreads across the floor and soaks up into the walls.

That is why the first questions are about your house. When you call or send the form, tell us where the water came from, which rooms are wet, and how long it has been there. Tell us if the house has a basement, a crawl space, or a slab. If you are not sure, say so. That is enough to start.

Storms do some damage too. Trees came down across Lawrenceville in a January 2022 winter storm, and in 2018 one went through a living room. A damaged roof can leak into the attic for days before the stain shows up on the ceiling.

## Where people say they live

Lawrenceville is a lot of different places. These are the ones people name.

**Downtown.** Around the Square and the Lawn. It has older houses and newer apartments and town homes, and many people here rent. Court is at the Gwinnett Justice and Administration Center on Langley Drive, not the old courthouse on the Square.

**Collins Hill.** Mostly 1990s brick two-story houses.

**Sugarloaf Mills and Sugarloaf Parkway.** Houses and some town homes from the 1990s and 2000s.

**The lake neighborhoods.** Edgewater was built between 1989 and 2000. Chandler Pond and Knollwood Lakes are from the 1990s. River Colony is from the late 1980s. Great River at Tribble Mill is newer, from the 2000s.

**Old Norcross Road, and the south and west side.** An older corridor with a mix of houses. This is the part of the city that floods before the rest.

ZIP codes here include 30043, 30044, 30045, and 30046.

## Common causes in Lawrenceville

- **Water heater.** The tank leaks or lets go in a garage or closet. This is common in houses from the late 1980s through the 1990s.
- **Refrigerator or washer line.** The hose cracks. Water slides under the floor, and you smell it before you see it.
- **Old pipes.** Some houses from the late 1980s and early 1990s may still have gray plastic pipe that is known to fail. See the question below.
- **Burst pipe.** After a hard freeze, a pipe can split in a wall or an attic. Gwinnett had a lot of these in December 2022.
- **Creek water.** In south and west Lawrenceville, heavy rain can push water out of a creek and into a yard, a garage, or a house.
- **Storm damage.** A tree or wind takes off shingles. Rain gets in through the roof, a window, or a door.
- **Sewage, a sagging ceiling, or wet outlets.** Do not clean these up yourself. Call.

## If water is in the house now

1. Shut the water off, if you can do it safely. Stay away from wet outlets.
2. Take photos of the water, the room, and where it started.
3. Sewage, a sagging ceiling, or wet outlets: call (404) 882-7393 or send the form.
4. Already stopped a small drip? Use the [small-leak steps](/small-leaks).

Call your insurance company when you can. If you plan to file a claim, do not start cutting out drywall first.

Do not wait until tomorrow to dry things out. Wet drywall and flooring get worse overnight, and mold can start in 24 to 48 hours.

## Questions from Lawrenceville homeowners

**Water is coming up from the street or around my water meter. Who do I call?**
Call Gwinnett County's Department of Water Resources. It has run the water system in Lawrenceville since December 2020. If water is also inside your house, shut off the main valve, take photos, and call us about the wet part inside.

**Creek water got into my garage or yard. Is that the same as a burst pipe?**
No. Water from outside is dirty water, and it is often covered differently in an insurance policy. Do not walk through it, and stay away from wet outlets. Take photos of the water line on the walls and call. Ask your insurer whether your policy covers outside water.

**My house was built in the late 1980s. Should I worry about the pipes?**
Look at the pipes where they come out of the wall near the water heater. If they are gray plastic, that may be polybutylene, which was used in many houses until the mid-1990s and is known to fail. You cannot tell by looking at the neighborhood, so ask a plumber about your house. Do not assume it either way.

**A tree fell on my roof last night and it is raining inside. Do I wait for the roof first?**
No. Water that gets into the attic soaks the insulation and then the ceiling. Take photos, put buckets down, and call about the water now. Fix the roof as soon as you can, and tell your insurer about both.

**I rent downtown and the ceiling is leaking. Who do I call?**
Call your landlord or property manager first, because they own the building and often have their own insurance and a company they use. Take photos of the leak and of anything of yours that got wet. Your own renters insurance covers your belongings, not the building. Tell us what you know when you call.

Questions about drying time, insurance, or whether you have to hire the company that calls are answered on [FAQ](/faq) and [Insurance](/insurance).

## Shared (keep short on this URL)

**What a job looks like.** A crew checks the house, pulls out standing water, dries the structure, and replaces what can't be saved. You should be shown moisture readings, not just told it looks dry. How long it takes depends on the job. [How it works](/how-it-works).

**Clean water, dirty water, sewage.** A broken supply line is not the same as a washer overflow or a sewer backup. Say which one it is on the form.

**Insurance.** A sudden burst pipe or a failed appliance is often covered. A slow leak, outside water, or sewage may not be unless you bought extra coverage. Take photos first. Don't tear out drywall to "help." Ask your insurer. [Insurance](/insurance).
