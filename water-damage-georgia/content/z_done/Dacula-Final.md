## Water damage in Dacula

Most homes in Dacula were built in the late 1990s and early 2000s. That makes them 20 to 30 years old. This usually means the list of what can go wrong is getting longer too.

So the usual call is not a flood. It is a wet garage, a soft kitchen floor, or a stain on the ceiling. A burst pipe or a leaking appliance does most of the damage here.

Water does not always stay where it lands. On a slab, it spreads across the floor and soaks up into the walls. Over a crawl space, it can run underneath where you can't see it. In a finished basement, a leak upstairs can come down through the ceiling.

That is why the first questions are about your house. When you call or send the form, tell us where the water came from, which rooms are wet, and how long it has been there. Tell us if the house is on a slab, over a crawl space, or has a basement. If you are not sure, say so. That is enough to start.

Storms cause less damage here than leaks do, but they still do some. A hard rain can push water in at a window, a sliding door, or a bad spot in the roof. Wind and falling trees do the same. Dacula had trees down in a January 2022 winter storm, and hail the size of baseballs fell nearby in 2018. Water gets in through damaged shingles and flashing, and the stain on the ceiling can show up days later.

## Common causes in Dacula

- **Burst pipe.** After a hard freeze, a pipe can split in a wall or an attic. Gwinnett had a lot of these in December 2022.
- **Water heater.** The tank leaks or lets go in a garage or closet. This is common in houses from the late 1990s and early 2000s.
- **Refrigerator or washer line.** The hose cracks. Water slides under the floor, and you smell it before you see it.
- **A leak that reaches the basement.** Water from upstairs runs down into a finished basement. Basement flood cleanup takes longer than a one-room leak.
- **Storm water.** Rain gets in through a window, a sliding door, or a roof damaged by wind or a falling tree.
- **Sewage, a sagging ceiling, or wet outlets.** Do not clean these up yourself. Call.

## Questions from Dacula homeowners

**My water heater leaked in the garage and the floor looks dry now. Do I still need to call?**
Check before you decide. Water from a water heater runs under the wall and into the bottom of the drywall. Press on the baseboard and the lower wall. Look at the carpet and pad in the room next to the garage. If anything is soft, stained, or smells damp, call. If it only hit bare concrete and is dry, you probably caught it in time.

**Water from upstairs is coming through my basement ceiling. What do I do?**
Shut off the water if you can. Then stay out of the basement if the ceiling is sagging, a light fixture is dripping, or the floor is wet near outlets. Water can follow wiring and pool in the drywall above you. Take photos, then call. Finished basements take longer to dry, because the carpet, pad, and walls all hold water.

**Rain is coming in at my sliding door. Is that water damage?**
Yes. Water that comes in through a door, a window, or the roof can soak the floor and the wall under it. Take photos, put towels down, and call if it reached carpet, wood, or drywall. Storm water is often treated differently than a burst pipe in an insurance policy, so ask your insurer about it.

**My house was built around 2000 off 316. What usually breaks?**
The water heater, the refrigerator line, and the washer hose. If the water is off and you can see where it dripped, start with the [small-leak steps](/small-leaks). If the floor is still wet, call.

**It is 2 a.m. and the leak is stopped. Should I call now or wait until morning?**
Call now if water is still coming in, there is sewage, or the ceiling is sagging. If the leak is stopped and it is one small area, take photos, pick up what you can, and call first thing in the morning. Do not leave wet carpet and drywall for days.

**Can I stay in my house?**
Often yes, if it is a clean-water leak in one room. Stay out if there is sewage, a wet electrical panel, or a ceiling that is coming down.

Questions about drying time, insurance, or whether you have to hire the company that calls are answered on [FAQ](/faq) and [Insurance](/insurance).
