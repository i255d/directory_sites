## What Causes Water Damage in Lawrenceville

**Creek and heavy-rain flooding.** Wolf Creek runs through Lawrenceville and feeds the Yellow River, and Pew Creek crosses the area near Patterson Road. During Hurricane Helene in 2024, Gwinnett County officials flagged Wolf Creek as a high flood risk if more than six inches of rain fell over 24 to 48 hours, and Pew Creek at Patterson Road was reported flooding. County concerns that week centered more on the south and west sides of the city than on the Collins Hill and Sugarloaf side. Lawrenceville averages roughly 53 inches of rain a year, so even ordinary storms can overwhelm drainage on low lots.

**Freeze-related pipe bursts.** Georgia cold snaps catch homes off guard. During the December 2022 freeze, Gwinnett's 911 center saw a surge in burst-pipe calls, and arctic air returned in January 2025. Pipes in attics, exterior walls, and unheated crawl spaces are the usual victims.

**Aging plumbing and appliances.** Edgewater (built between 1989 and 2000), River Colony, and other late-1980s and 1990s neighborhoods are now old enough that original water heaters, washing-machine hoses, and supply lines fail regularly. Homes built in the late 1980s may also fall in the era when polybutylene pipe was used, a material with a reputation for failing, so ask a plumber what's actually in your house rather than assuming.

**Basements and crawl spaces.** Many lake and hillside streets have basements, while older downtown homes are more likely to have crawl spaces. The foundation changes where water collects and how long a leak can go unnoticed.

**Storm damage.** Winter storms and spring thunderstorms have brought trees down on Lawrenceville homes and roofs, including during a January 2022 winter storm. A damaged roof can leak into the attic for days before a stain shows up on the ceiling.

## What Happens When You Call

1. **Call or send the form.** Tell them your address, what kind of water it is, and which rooms are affected.
2. **Talk it through.** The restoration company will ask what happened, where the water went, and whether you have insurance.
3. **Inspection.** A good crew checks for hidden moisture in walls, ceilings, flooring, and under cabinets, not just what you can see.
4. **Water removal and drying.** Pumps and extractors take out standing water. Air movers and dehumidifiers then run for days, and the crew should show you moisture readings, not just say it looks dry.
5. **Cleanup and repairs.** Depending on the damage, this can include removing wet pad, drywall, insulation, or flooring.

You're never obligated to hire anyone, and you're free to get a second opinion before any work starts.

## Quick Steps While You Wait

- If it's safe, shut off the water at your main valve.
- Stay out of rooms with wet outlets, a sagging ceiling, or sewage.
- Photograph the damage before you move or throw anything away.
- Call your insurance carrier when you can.
- Don't start cutting drywall if you plan to file a claim.

## Insurance and Water Damage in Lawrenceville

Many homeowners policies cover sudden events like a burst pipe or an appliance failure. Slow leaks that went unaddressed, flooding from creeks or heavy runoff, and sewer backup may not be covered unless you bought those add-ons. Only your policy can answer that, so check with your carrier. A restoration company that works with insurance jobs can document the damage and talk with your adjuster.

## Lawrenceville Water Damage FAQ

**How fast will someone respond?**
It depends on the time of day and how many storms are hitting Gwinnett that night. Ask when you call. Emergency water is worth a call right away, because drying sooner almost always costs less.

**Can I stay in my house?**
Often yes for a clean-water leak in one room. Stay out if there's sewage, a wet electrical panel, or a ceiling coming down.

**The water is coming from the street or my meter. Who do I call?**
Report it to Gwinnett County's Department of Water Resources, which has handled water service in Lawrenceville since December 2020. If water is inside your home, shut off your main valve and call a restoration company.

**How soon can mold start?**
Mold can begin to develop within 24 to 48 hours if wet materials sit, which is why waiting until morning can make the job bigger.
