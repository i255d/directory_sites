## Water damage in Snellville

Ask anyone in Snellville for directions and they will say 124. The road is also called Scenic Highway. The town grew up around the corner of 124 and Highway 78, and for a long time it had no real downtown. The Grove at Towne Center is the new one. Briscoe Park, off Lenora Church Road, is the old civic park.

Most of the houses are older than the Grove. A lot of Snellville was built in the 1980s and 1990s, so many homes are 30 to 45 years old. Water heaters have been replaced, or should have been. Washer hoses and supply lines have been there for decades. So the usual call is a leak. It is a water heater, a fridge line, or a slow drip under a bathroom floor that nobody saw.

What is under the house matters. Many 1980s ranches and split-levels sit over a crawl space. Lake and hillside lots more often have a basement. Water goes to different places in each, and a crawl space can hold a leak for a long time before anything shows inside.

That is why the first questions are about your house. When you call or send the form, tell us where the water came from, which rooms are wet, and how long it has been there. Tell us if the house has a basement, a crawl space, or a slab. If you are not sure, say so. That is enough to start.

Snellville also has creeks. During Hurricane Helene in 2024, Big Haynes Creek at Lenora Road and No Business Creek at Lee Road both went over flood stage, and so did the Yellow River at Highway 124. If you live near one of these creeks, outside water is something to think about.

Storms do the rest. In a January 2022 storm, trees and wires came down on roads around Snellville, and a limb went through a roof. A damaged roof can leak into the attic for days before the stain shows up on the ceiling.

## Where people say they live

Snellville has two ZIP codes. 30078 covers most of the town and the Grove. 30039 is the south side, around Norris Lake.

**The Grove and Towne Center.** New, mostly apartments and mixed-use buildings. This is a different kind of building from the rest of the town.

**Briscoe Park area.** Split-level, ranch, and two-story houses from the 1980s and 1990s.

**Lenora.** 1980s and 1990s houses on larger lots. Some are in an HOA and some are not.

## Common causes in Snellville

- **Water heater.** The tank leaks or lets go in a garage or closet. This is common in houses from the 1980s and 1990s.
- **Refrigerator or washer line.** The hose cracks. Water slides under the floor, and you smell it before you see it.
- **Slow leak in a crawl space.** A supply line or drain drips for weeks. The insulation hangs down wet and the floor above gets soft.
- **Old pipes.** Some houses from the 1980s and early 1990s may still have gray plastic pipe that is known to fail. See the question below.
- **Burst pipe.** After a hard freeze, a pipe can split in a wall or an attic. Gwinnett had a lot of these in December 2022.
- **Storm water.** Rain gets in through a window, a door, or a roof damaged by wind or a falling tree. Near a creek, it can come in from outside.
- **Sewage, a sagging ceiling, or wet outlets.** Do not clean these up yourself. Call.

## If water is in the house now

1. Shut the water off, if you can do it safely. Stay away from wet outlets.
2. Take photos of the water, the room, and where it started.
3. Sewage, a sagging ceiling, or wet outlets: call (404) 882-7393 or send the form.
4. Already stopped a small drip? Use the [small-leak steps](/small-leaks).

Call your insurance company when you can. If you plan to file a claim, do not start cutting out drywall first.

Do not wait until tomorrow to dry things out. Wet drywall and flooring get worse overnight, and mold can start in 24 to 48 hours.

## Questions from Snellville homeowners

**There is water in my crawl space after a hard rain. Is it a leak or the weather?**
Look at where it is coming from. Water that comes in at the edge of the wall or from the ground after rain is outside water. Water near a pipe, the water heater, or the floor above is a leak. Either way, wet insulation and a soft floor above mean the job is bigger than it looks. Do not go in if there are wires in the water. Take photos from the access door and call. Outside water and pipe leaks are often covered differently in an insurance policy, so ask your insurer.

**We just bought a house from the 1980s. What should we check before something leaks?**
Find the main shut-off, and show everyone in the house where it is. Look at the water heater for rust, drips, or a puddle, and find out how old it is. Look at the washer hoses and the line to the refrigerator. Look at the pipes near the water heater. If they are gray plastic, that may be polybutylene, which was used in many houses until the mid-1990s and is known to fail. You cannot tell from the neighborhood, so ask a plumber about your house.

**The apartment above mine at the Grove overflowed and water is coming through my ceiling. What do I do?**
Take photos of the ceiling, the walls, and your belongings before you move anything. Write down the time you first saw it. Tell the property manager in writing, not just by phone, so there is a record. Do not argue about whose fault it is. That gets sorted out later. Stay out from under a sagging ceiling, and call if the water keeps coming.

**We got a boil-water notice. Is that a water damage problem?**
No. A boil-water notice is about drinking water, and Gwinnett County's Department of Water Resources handles it. The Norris Lake area had one in March 2020 after a main break. It becomes a restoration call only if water got into your house, such as from a main break that flooded a yard, a basement, or a crawl space. If it did, shut off the main valve, take photos, and call.

Questions about drying time, insurance, or whether you have to hire the company that calls are answered on [FAQ](/faq) and [Insurance](/insurance).

## Shared (keep short on this URL)

**What a job looks like.** A crew checks the house, pulls out standing water, dries the structure, and replaces what can't be saved. You should be shown moisture readings, not just told it looks dry. How long it takes depends on the job. [How it works](/how-it-works).

**Clean water, dirty water, sewage.** A broken supply line is not the same as a washer overflow or a sewer backup. Say which one it is on the form.

**Insurance.** A sudden burst pipe or a failed appliance is often covered. A slow leak, outside water, or sewage may not be unless you bought extra coverage. Take photos first. Don't tear out drywall to "help." Ask your insurer. [Insurance](/insurance).
