## Water damage in Winder

Winder is the county seat of Barrow County, not a Gwinnett suburb. It started out as Jug Tavern, and downtown is still around the courthouse square and Broad Street. Fort Yargo State Park is the backyard lake, and you can walk there from downtown on the Wilkins Greenway.

Downtown is old. It has cottages and mill-town houses, and many sit over a crawl space. New builder houses are going up on the edge of town, like the Hills at Cedar Creek. Along Atlanta Highway and Auburn Road, toward Gwinnett, it is a mix of older houses, businesses, and some newer ones.

Where you live changes what you have. Houses in town are usually on the City of Winder's water and sewer. Outside the city, houses are more often on a well and septic. A crawl space is common outside town too.

Houses from the late 1970s to the mid-1990s that were remodeled may have gray plastic pipe, called polybutylene, which is known to fail. The new houses do not. You cannot tell from the neighborhood, so ask a plumber if you are not sure.

That is why the first questions are about your house. When you call or send the form, tell us where the water came from, which rooms are wet, and how long it has been there. Tell us if the house has a crawl space, a basement, or a slab, and whether you are on city water or a well.

Winder is outside Gwinnett, where this site started. The companies that answer will tell you if they can come to your address. If they cannot, we do not pretend they can.

Storms do some damage. During Hurricane Helene in 2024, Barrow County had about 6 to 7 inches of rain, and trees and power lines came down in the area. A damaged roof can leak into the attic for days before the stain shows up on the ceiling.

## Common causes in Winder

- **Water heater.** The tank leaks or lets go in a garage or closet.
- **Refrigerator or washer line.** The hose cracks. Water slides under the floor, and you smell it before you see it.
- **Slow leak in a crawl space.** A supply line or drain drips for weeks. The insulation hangs down wet and the floor above gets soft.
- **Gray plastic pipe.** Some remodeled houses from the late 1970s to the mid-1990s may have polybutylene pipe that is known to fail.
- **Burst pipe.** After a hard freeze, a pipe can split in a wall or an attic. Gwinnett and Walton had many in December 2022.
- **Storm damage.** Rain gets in through a window, a door, or a roof damaged by wind or a falling tree.
- **Septic backup.** Outside the city, wastewater can back up into a tub or floor drain. This is sewage.
- **Sewage, a sagging ceiling, or wet outlets.** Do not clean these up yourself. Call.

## If water is in the house now

1. Shut the water off, if you can do it safely. Stay away from wet outlets.
2. Take photos of the water, the room, and where it started.
3. Sewage, a sagging ceiling, or wet outlets: call (404) 882-7393 or send the form.
4. Already stopped a small drip? Use the [small-leak steps](/small-leaks).

Call your insurance company when you can. If you plan to file a claim, do not start cutting out drywall first.

Do not wait until tomorrow to dry things out. Wet drywall and flooring get worse overnight, and mold can start in 24 to 48 hours.

## Questions from Winder homeowners

**Will someone come out to Winder?**
It depends on the company and on where in Winder you are. The companies that answer will tell you if they can come to your address. Give your street and ZIP, and say if you are in town or outside it. If no one can come, we will say so, and you can call a local Barrow County company.

**Water is coming up near my meter or in the street. Who do I call?**
In Winder, call the City of Winder Water Department. It is not the Gwinnett County water department. If you are outside the city and on a well, the problem is yours or your well company's. If water is inside your house, shut off the main valve, take photos, and call us about the wet part inside.

**My house is outside the city, on a well and septic. Does that change anything?**
Yes. A septic backup is sewage, and it needs a septic company for the cause and cleanup for the inside. A well pump or pressure tank that leaks is a well company's job, and drying the house is ours. Tell us which you have when you call.

**I have an older house downtown with plaster walls and wood floors. Does that change how it is dried?**
It can. Old plaster and hardwood take water differently from new drywall and vinyl. Tell us the age of the house and what the walls and floors are made of, and do not tear anything out yourself. Take photos, and call.
