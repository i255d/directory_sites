## Water damage in Hamilton Mill

Hamilton Mill sits off I-85 at Exit 120. Your mail may say Dacula, because the ZIP code is 30019. The area is also now the City of Mulberry, which voters approved in 2024. But people who live here say Hamilton Mill, and it is its own place.

Homes here started going up in the mid-1990s and kept going for years. That makes many of them 20 to 30 years old. Water heaters, roofs, and air conditioners from the first years are now on their second life, or past it.

So the usual call is not a flood. It is a water heater that let go in the garage, a stain on the ceiling under an upstairs bathroom, or a wet spot on the carpet that nobody can explain.

In a two-story house, a leak upstairs does not stay upstairs. Water from a bathroom or laundry room can run through the floor and down the walls to the rooms below. By the time a stain shows on the ceiling, the damage has often spread past where the water started.

Water does not always stay where it lands. On a slab, it spreads across the floor and soaks up into the walls. In a house with a finished basement, a leak upstairs can come down through the ceiling and pool behind the drywall where you can't see it.

That is why the first questions are about your house. When you call or send the form, tell us where the water came from, which rooms are wet, and how long it has been there. Tell us if the house has a basement or is on a slab. If you are not sure, say so. That is enough to start.

Storms do some damage too. A hard rain can push water in at a window, a door, or a bad spot in the roof. In Georgia, most of the house damage from Hurricane Helene came from falling trees, not floodwater. A damaged roof can leak for days before the stain shows up on the ceiling.

## Common causes in Hamilton Mill

- **Water heater.** The tank leaks or lets go in a garage or closet. This is common in houses from the late 1990s and early 2000s.
- **Air conditioner drain.** The drain line clogs and the pan overflows. The water comes through the ceiling below it.
- **Refrigerator or washer line.** The hose cracks. Water slides under the floor, and you smell it before you see it.
- **Burst pipe.** After a hard freeze, a pipe can split in a wall or an attic. Gwinnett had a lot of these in December 2022.
- **Leak that reaches the basement.** Water from upstairs runs down into a finished basement. Basement cleanup takes longer than a one-room leak.
- **Storm water.** Rain gets in through a window, a door, or a roof damaged by wind or a falling tree.
- **Sewage, a sagging ceiling, or wet outlets.** Do not clean these up yourself. Call.

Do not wait until tomorrow to dry things out. Wet drywall and flooring get worse overnight, and mold can start in 24 to 48 hours.

## Questions from Hamilton Mill homeowners

**I found a leak and I do not know where my main water shut-off is. What do I do?**
Look where the water line comes into the house. It is often in the garage, a basement, or a closet near the water heater. A round handle turns clockwise to close. A lever turns a quarter turn, until it sits across the pipe. If the leak is at one fixture, look for the small valve under the sink or behind the toilet first. If you cannot find any of them, say so when you call.

**There is a brown stain on my upstairs ceiling and no pipe is above it. What is it?**
It may be the air conditioner. When the drain line clogs, the water backs up and spills out of the pan, and it can run through the ceiling below. Turn the air conditioner off so it stops making more water. Take a photo, put a bucket under the drip, and call if the ceiling is wet, sagging, or the stain is spreading.

**My leak was upstairs. Do I need to check downstairs too?**
Yes. Water goes down through the floor and into the walls. Look at the ceiling in the room below and at the tops of the walls. Press on the drywall. If it is soft, stained, or bubbling, there is water behind it, even if the room feels dry. Tell us which room the leak started in.

**My basement floor is wet after a hard rain. Is it a leak or the weather?**
Look at where the water is coming from. If it comes through the wall or up through the floor after rain, it is outside water. If it is near a pipe, the water heater, or the ceiling, it is a leak. Either way it can soak carpet and drywall, so take photos and call. Outside water and burst pipes are often covered differently in an insurance policy, so ask your insurer.

**My water heater is the one that came with the house. Should I replace it before it leaks?**
It is a good question to ask before it becomes a 2 a.m. call. Tanks wear out. Look at the bottom for rust, drips, or a puddle. If it is the original and you are not sure how old it is, it is worth having a plumber look at it.

**We came home from a trip and a pipe froze and split while we were gone. What do we do?**
Shut off the water at the main. Take photos of everything before you move it. Then call. The longer water sits in carpet and drywall, the bigger the job gets, and a house that has been wet for days needs more than fans.
