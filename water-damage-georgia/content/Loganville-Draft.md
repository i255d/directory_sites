## Water damage in Loganville

Loganville has one ZIP code, 30052, and two counties. The city sits on the line between Gwinnett and Walton. Two neighbors with the same mailing address can be in different counties. In town, U.S. 78 is Atlanta Highway. Rosebud and the border with Grayson are on the Gwinnett side.

Many houses here have a basement or a terrace level, which is a finished level partly below ground. Water from outside collects there, and a leak upstairs can end up there too. That shows up more here than on a street of slab houses.

Houses from the 1980s to the mid-1990s may have gray plastic pipe, called polybutylene, which is known to fail. The newer ones, in Rosebud and in the 2000s subdivisions, are past that. In those, the water heater and the hoses are the usual trouble. You cannot tell from the neighborhood, so ask a plumber if you are not sure.

That is why the first questions are about your house. When you call or send the form, tell us where the water came from, which rooms are wet, and how long it has been there. Tell us if the house has a basement, a terrace level, a crawl space, or a slab. Tell us your street, and which county you think you are in.

Storms do some damage. During Hurricane Helene in 2024, power lines came down on Loganville Highway, and on the Walton side there was 6 to 7 inches of rain, with trees down and one on a house. A damaged roof can leak into the attic for days before the stain shows up on the ceiling.

## Where people say they live

**Downtown and U.S. 78.** The older core and the Atlanta Highway strip. Older houses here are more likely to sit over a crawl space.

**Rosebud.** On the Gwinnett side. Houses from the 1990s and 2000s.

**Bay Creek and Sharon.** Established neighborhoods on the Walton side.

**Youth.** More rural, with larger lots. More septic, and more crawl spaces.

**Ozora.** Newer houses, on the line with Grayson.

**Westgate Estates.** New builder houses from the 2020s.

## Common causes in Loganville

- **Basement or terrace-level water.** Water from outside, or a leak upstairs, ends up on the lower level. A cleanup there takes longer than a one-room leak.
- **Water heater.** The tank leaks or lets go in a garage, closet, or basement.
- **Refrigerator or washer line.** The hose cracks. Water slides under the floor, and you smell it before you see it.
- **Gray plastic pipe.** Some houses from the 1980s to the mid-1990s may have polybutylene pipe that is known to fail.
- **Burst pipe.** After a hard freeze, a pipe can split in a wall or an attic. Gwinnett and Walton had a lot of these in December 2022.
- **Storm and lightning damage.** Rain gets in through a roof damaged by wind or a falling tree. A lightning strike can start a fire, and then there is water from putting it out.
- **Sewage, a sagging ceiling, or wet outlets.** Do not clean these up yourself. Call.

## If water is in the house now

1. Shut the water off, if you can do it safely. Stay away from wet outlets.
2. Take photos of the water, the room, and where it started.
3. Sewage, a sagging ceiling, or wet outlets: call (404) 882-7393 or send the form.
4. Already stopped a small drip? Use the [small-leak steps](/small-leaks).

Call your insurance company when you can. If you plan to file a claim, do not start cutting out drywall first.

Do not wait until tomorrow to dry things out. Wet drywall and flooring get worse overnight, and mold can start in 24 to 48 hours.

## Questions from Loganville homeowners

**My sump pump stopped working in a storm and the basement is wet. What do I do?**
Do not step into standing water in the basement to check the pump. The wiring may be live. If you can reach the breaker without standing in water, turn off the power to the basement. Take photos of the water line and what got wet, and call. Tell us it is a sump pump or outside water, since that is handled differently from a pipe leak.

**What does terrace level mean, and why do you ask?**
A terrace level is a finished level partly below ground, often with a door out to the yard. Water from outside collects there, and a leak upstairs can run down into it. When you call, tell us if you have one, and which rooms are on it. That changes how the job is done.

**Does it matter which county I am in?**
It can. The Gwinnett and Walton line runs through Loganville, and the county can change who handles your water and some local rules. It also can change which company comes out. Give your street and ZIP, and say which county you think you are in. If a company cannot come to your address, we will say so.

**Lightning hit my house, there was a fire, and now the first floor is soaked. Who do I call?**
Call 911 first if there is any fire or smoke. Once the fire department is done, the water used to put out the fire is water damage, and we can help with that. Smoke and fire cleanup is a different job. Tell us what happened when you call so the right company answers. Take photos before anything is moved.

Questions about drying time, insurance, or whether you have to hire the company that calls are answered on [FAQ](/faq) and [Insurance](/insurance).
